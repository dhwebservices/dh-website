Read this first

  • We're setting up Valleys Digital as a CIO (charitable incorporated organisation) using the Charity Commission's own foundation model constitution. A CIO is a proper registered charity from day one, and the trustees aren't personally liable for its debts. It only exists once the Charity Commission registers it. Registering is free, and usually takes about 40 working days when the Commission's model is used.
  • "Foundation" means the trustees run it. There are no voting members or member AGMs.
  • We need at least 3 trustees, and fewer than half of them may be connected to DH (that's the law, because the charity may pay DH to build sites). David counts as connected. You're not legally "connected", but you work with DH, so we treat you the same way. So we need at least two more trustees with no link to DH who aren't related to either of you.
  • Until the Commission registers us, don't say "registered charity", don't use a charity number, and don't take any money for the charity.
  • Never give Claude, or anyone else, a bank login, card number, PIN or one-time code. Banks and forms that ask for those are yours or David's to fill in.
  • Don't pay for anything or buy anything (a domain name, insurance, a fee) without David saying yes first.
  • Before you send any email, letter or message for the charity, show David the exact words and get his OK.

Register the CIO with the Charity Commission

About 2 hours to apply, then about 40 working days (around 2 months) to wait

This is the step that creates the charity. A CIO has to register whatever its income, so we can apply straight away, with no need to wait for a grant. It's free. Don't use any website that charges to "register a charity".

Before applying (check each one)

  1. At least 3 trustees, with the legal checks done and declarations signed. Fewer than half connected to DH.
  2. The completed constitution, with the trustees' names in clause 9(4). Don't change any wording: it's the Commission's model, which is what gets us the faster decision. If anything must change, ask Claude first.
  3. A short, plain description of what we'll do, who benefits and how the public benefits. Ask Claude to write it from the plan.
  4. Our plans for money: the Awards for All bid (about £8,000 for 10 sites), local sponsors and donations.
  5. The charity's email and correspondence address.

Applying

  1. Read gov.uk's "How to register your charity (CC21b)" and "Charitable incorporated organisations" guidance first.
  2. David (or the chosen applicant from Meeting A) starts the online application at gov.uk ("Register your charity"). Choose CIO, and say the constitution is the Commission's foundation model. Upload the constitution.
  3. Every trustee gets an email and confirms their own details and declaration online. Nobody fills one in for someone else.
  4. Declare the link with DH. The application asks about trustee benefits. The answer:
    • the charity may contract DH Website Services, which is connected to a trustee, to build sites;
    • this would be under section 185 of the Charities Act 2011 and constitution clause 6(2)(b);
    • it would be at or below DH's public prices, approved only by trustees with no DH connection, with fewer than half of the trustees connected; and
    • it would be declared to funders.
    The conflicts of interest policy explains all this. Have it ready to upload if asked.
  5. Reply to any questions from the Commission quickly, within the deadline they give. Send their questions to David and Claude first.

Staying legal every year

A few hours a year: put the dates in the calendar

Being set up properly isn't the end. These are the things the law (and our constitution) expects every year. Missing them can get the charity flagged on the public register.

Every year

  1. Accounts and trustees' annual report at the end of each financial year. Simple "receipts and payments" accounts are fine while income is small. Ask Claude to draft them from the bank statements.
  2. Annual return, accounts and annual report to the Charity Commission within 10 months of the year end. As a CIO, we must send all three every year, whatever our income (constitution clause 25). Missing the deadline shows up publicly on the register.
  3. Independent examination of the accounts, by someone independent of the trustees, once income goes over the legal threshold (£25,000, rising to £40,000 for years ending on or after 30 September 2026; check gov.uk), or whenever a funder asks for one.
  4. Trustee terms: trustees serve 3-year terms (the first ones are staggered: 4, 3 and 2 years). New trustees are appointed at a trustees' meeting (clause 10), after the legal checks. Keep the combined register of members and trustees up to date (clause 23).
  5. Keep the register up to date: tell the Commission within 28 days if trustees, contact details or the bank change. Re-do the disqualification checks for every new trustee.
  6. Review the policies (conflicts, safeguarding, data protection, equality), and update the register of interests.
  7. Report to funders on time, and spend grants only on what the bid said.

Rules to keep

  • Fundraising: follow the Fundraising Regulator's Code of Fundraising Practice. Collecting money in the street or door to door needs a licence from RCT Council first.
  • Gift Aid: only claim it with a proper signed declaration from the donor. Keep the records for 6 years.
  • Report serious incidents (fraud, theft, a safeguarding incident, a data breach) to the Charity Commission promptly. Search gov.uk for "Reporting a serious incident".
  • Never mix charity money with DH's or anyone's personal money.
  • On every letter, email, invoice, receipt and the website: our name, the words "charitable incorporated organisation" (because our name doesn't include "CIO") and the registered charity number.
  • Any change to the constitution needs a 75% vote of the trustees (as members) and must be sent to the Charity Commission within 15 days. Changes to the objects, to what happens on closure, or to trustee benefits need the Commission's permission first (clause 28).

Constitution (Charity Commission CIO foundation model) 
# Constitution of Valleys Digital

**Charitable Incorporated Organisation whose only voting members are its charity trustees (Charity Commission "Foundation" model constitution, February 2024 version)**

> **DRAFT. About this document:** This is the Charity Commission's own model constitution for a "foundation" CIO. It has been completed for Valleys Digital, which is a working name. Following the model's own notes, we have:
> - chosen the options;
> - filled in the blanks;
> - removed the clauses that don't apply (Scotland and Northern Ireland wording, ex-officio and nominated trustees, the seal); and
> - added the model's optional wording on electronic communications.
>
> **No wording of our own has been added.** That keeps it eligible for the Commission's faster route: a decision in about 40 working days when model wording is used. The Charity Commission model text is Crown copyright, reused under the Open Government Licence v3.0.
>
> **Still to fill in before applying:**
> - the names of the first trustees (clause 9(4)); and
> - the final name, if it changes.

Date of constitution (last amended): ____________ *(leave blank until registered)*

## 1. Name
The name of the Charitable Incorporated Organisation ("the CIO") is **Valleys Digital**.

## 2. National location of principal office
The CIO must have a principal office in England or Wales. The principal office of the CIO is in **Wales**.

## 3. Objects
The objects of the CIO are, for the public benefit, in Rhondda Cynon Taf and the surrounding area:

1. to promote the efficiency and effectiveness of charities and other organisations established for charitable purposes, and the effective use of charitable resources, in particular by providing websites, digital tools, digital support and training; and
2. to advance the education of the public, in particular people who are digitally excluded by reason of age, disability, low income or other disadvantage, in the use of digital technology and online services, including how to use them safely.

## 4. Powers
The CIO has power to do anything which is calculated to further its objects or is conducive or incidental to doing so. In particular, the CIO has power to:

1. borrow money and to charge the whole or any part of its property as security for the repayment of the money borrowed. The CIO must comply as appropriate with sections 124 and 125 of the Charities Act 2011, if it wishes to mortgage land;
2. buy, take on lease or in exchange, hire or otherwise acquire any property and to maintain and equip it for use;
3. sell, lease or otherwise dispose of all or any part of the property belonging to the CIO. In exercising this power, the CIO must comply as appropriate with sections 117 and 119-123 of the Charities Act 2011;
4. employ and remunerate such staff as are necessary for carrying out the work of the CIO. The CIO may employ or remunerate a charity trustee only to the extent that it is permitted to do so by clause 6 (Benefits and payments to charity trustees and connected persons) and provided it complies with the conditions of that clause;
5. deposit or invest funds, employ a professional fund-manager, and arrange for the investments or other property of the CIO to be held in the name of a nominee, in the same manner and subject to the same conditions as the trustees of a trust are permitted to do by the Trustee Act 2000.

## 5. Application of income and property
1. The income and property of the CIO must be applied solely towards the promotion of the objects.
   1. A charity trustee is entitled to be reimbursed from the property of the CIO or may pay out of such property reasonable expenses properly incurred by him or her when acting on behalf of the CIO.
   2. A charity trustee may benefit from trustee indemnity insurance cover purchased at the CIO's expense in accordance with, and subject to the conditions in, section 189 of the Charities Act 2011.
2. None of the income or property of the CIO may be paid or transferred directly or indirectly by way of dividend, bonus or otherwise by way of profit to any member of the CIO.
3. Nothing in this clause shall prevent a charity trustee or connected person receiving any benefit or payment which is authorised by Clause 6.

## 6. Benefits and payments to charity trustees and connected persons
1. **General provisions.** No charity trustee or connected person may:
   1. buy or receive any goods or services from the CIO on terms preferential to those applicable to members of the public;
   2. sell goods, services, or any interest in land to the CIO;
   3. be employed by, or receive any remuneration from, the CIO;
   4. receive any other financial benefit from the CIO;

   unless the payment or benefit is permitted by sub-clause (2) of this clause or authorised by the court or the prior written consent of the Charity Commission ("the Commission") has been obtained. In this clause, a "financial benefit" means a benefit, direct or indirect, which is either money or has a monetary value.
2. **Scope and powers permitting trustees' or connected persons' benefits**
   1. A charity trustee or connected person may receive a benefit from the CIO as a beneficiary of the CIO provided that a majority of the trustees do not benefit in this way.
   2. A charity trustee or connected person may enter into a contract for the supply of services and/or goods to the CIO where that is permitted in accordance with, and subject to the conditions in, sections 185 to 188 of the Charities Act 2011.
   3. A charity trustee or connected person may receive interest on money lent to the CIO at a reasonable and proper rate which must be not more than the Bank of England bank rate (also known as the base rate).
   4. A charity trustee or connected person may receive rent for premises let by the trustee or connected person to the CIO. The amount of the rent and the other terms of the lease must be reasonable and proper. The charity trustee concerned must withdraw from any meeting at which such a proposal or the rent or other terms of the lease are under discussion.
   5. A charity trustee or connected person may take part in the normal trading and fundraising activities of the CIO on the same terms as members of the public.
3. In sub-clause (2) of this clause:
   1. "the CIO" includes any company in which the CIO:
      1. holds more than 50% of the shares; or
      2. controls more than 50% of the voting rights attached to the shares; or
      3. has the right to appoint one or more directors to the board of the company;
   2. "connected person" includes any person within the definition set out in clause 30 (Interpretation).

## 7. Conflicts of interest and conflicts of loyalty
A charity trustee must:

1. declare the nature and extent of any interest, direct or indirect, which he or she has in a proposed transaction or arrangement with the CIO or in any transaction or arrangement entered into by the CIO which has not previously been declared; and
2. absent himself or herself from any discussions of the charity trustees in which it is possible that a conflict of interest will arise between his or her duty to act solely in the interests of the CIO and any personal interest (including but not limited to any financial interest).

Any charity trustee absenting himself or herself from any discussions in accordance with this clause must not vote or be counted as part of the quorum in any decision of the charity trustees on the matter.

## 8. Liability of members to contribute to the assets of the CIO if it is wound up
If the CIO is wound up, the members of the CIO have no liability to contribute to its assets and no personal responsibility for settling its debts and liabilities.

## 9. Charity trustees
1. **Functions and duties of charity trustees.** The charity trustees shall manage the affairs of the CIO and may for that purpose exercise all the powers of the CIO. It is the duty of each charity trustee:
   1. to exercise his or her powers and to perform his or her functions in his or her capacity as a trustee of the CIO in the way he or she decides in good faith would be most likely to further the purposes of the CIO; and
   2. to exercise, in the performance of those functions, such care and skill as is reasonable in the circumstances having regard in particular to:
      1. any special knowledge or experience that he or she has or holds himself or herself out as having; and,
      2. if he or she acts as a charity trustee of the CIO in the course of a business or profession, to any special knowledge or experience that it is reasonable to expect of a person acting in the course of that kind of business or profession.
2. **Eligibility for trusteeship**
   1. Every charity trustee must be a natural person.
   2. No individual may be appointed as a charity trustee of the CIO:
      1. if he or she is under the age of 16 years; or
      2. if he or she would automatically cease to hold office under the provisions of clause 12(1)(e).
   3. No one is entitled to act as a charity trustee whether on appointment or on any re-appointment until he or she has expressly acknowledged, in whatever way the charity trustees decide, his or her acceptance of the office of charity trustee.
   4. At least one of the trustees of the CIO must be 18 years of age or over. If there is no trustee aged at least 18 years, the remaining trustees may only act to call a meeting of the charity trustees, or appoint a new charity trustee.
3. **Number of charity trustees**
   1. There must be at least three charity trustees. If the number falls below this minimum, the remaining trustee or trustees may act only to call a meeting of the charity trustees, or appoint a new charity trustee.
   2. The maximum number of charity trustees is 12. The charity trustees may not appoint any charity trustee if as a result the number of charity trustees would exceed the maximum.
4. **First charity trustees.** The first charity trustees are as follows, and are appointed for the following terms:
   - ______________________________ for 4 years
   - ______________________________ for 3 years
   - ______________________________ for 2 years
   - ______________________________ for 2 years *(optional fourth)*

## 10. Appointment of charity trustees
1. Apart from the first charity trustees, every trustee must be appointed for a term of three years by a resolution passed at a properly convened meeting of the charity trustees.
2. In selecting individuals for appointment as charity trustees, the charity trustees must have regard to the skills, knowledge and experience needed for the effective administration of the CIO.

## 11. Information for new charity trustees
The charity trustees will make available to each new charity trustee, on or before his or her first appointment:

1. a copy of the current version of this constitution; and
2. a copy of the CIO's latest Trustees' Annual Report and statement of accounts.

## 12. Retirement and removal of charity trustees
1. A charity trustee ceases to hold office if he or she:
   1. retires by notifying the CIO in writing (but only if enough charity trustees will remain in office when the notice of resignation takes effect to form a quorum for meetings);
   2. is absent without the permission of the charity trustees from all their meetings held within a period of six months and the trustees resolve that his or her office be vacated;
   3. dies;
   4. in the written opinion, given to the CIO, of a registered medical practitioner treating that person, has become physically or mentally incapable of acting as a trustee and may remain so for more than three months;
   5. is disqualified from acting as a charity trustee by virtue of sections 178-180 of the Charities Act 2011 (or any statutory re-enactment or modification of that provision).
2. Any person retiring as a charity trustee is eligible for reappointment.
3. A charity trustee who has served for three consecutive terms may not be reappointed for a fourth consecutive term but may be reappointed after an interval of at least one year.

## 13. Taking of decisions by charity trustees
Any decision may be taken either:

1. at a meeting of the charity trustees; or

# Conflicts of Interest Policy — Valleys Digital

DRAFT for the trustees to adopt at the first meeting.

## The legal basis
This policy applies:
- clause 7 of our constitution (conflicts of interest);
- clause 6 of our constitution (benefits and payments to trustees and connected persons); and
- sections 185 to 188 of the Charities Act 2011.

DH Website Services (David Hooper Home Limited) is a **connected person** in law, because a trustee has a substantial interest in it (constitution clause 30). Anyone else who works for DH is not legally "connected", but has a conflict of loyalty and follows this policy in the same way.

Under section 185, the charity may pay a trustee-connected supplier only if all of these apply:
- there is a written agreement setting the maximum amount;
- the other trustees are satisfied that it is in the charity's best interests;
- fewer than half of the trustees are covered by such agreements; and
- the constitution doesn't forbid it.

## Why this matters for us
One of our founders owns DH Website Services, the web company in Pontypridd that may be paid to build the websites we fund. That is allowed, but only if the decision is made by trustees with no connection to DH and is open to everyone. This policy is how we make sure of that. Funders and the Charity Commission will look for it.

## What counts as a conflict
A conflict exists when what's best for the charity could clash with what's best for:
- a trustee;
- a person connected to a trustee (family, people they live with); or
- a business connected to a trustee (one they own or direct, or work for).

It includes "conflicts of loyalty", for example a trustee who also sits on the committee of a group applying to us for a free website.

## What we do
1. **Register of interests.** Every trustee fills in the register when they join, and updates it whenever something changes. The secretary keeps it, and it is reviewed once a year, when the trustees approve the annual report.
2. **Declare at every meeting.** "Declarations of interest" is the second item on every agenda. Any interest is recorded in the minutes.
3. **Leave the room.** A trustee with a conflict on a matter leaves the discussion (or the online meeting) and does not vote. They may answer factual questions first if the other trustees ask.
4. **Quorum without them.** A decision on a conflicted matter needs at least two trustees with no connection to it.
5. **Payments to DH Website Services** (or any other trustee-connected supplier):
   - The work and its price are set in a written agreement before any work starts.
   - The price per site is no higher than DH's published public price for the same work. The trustees record a comparison with at least one other supplier's price.
   - The agreement is approved by trustees with no connection to DH.
   - Each invoice is checked against the agreement and approved by two trustees with no connection to DH before it is paid.
   - The arrangement is declared in every funding bid and every report to a funder.
   - Fewer than half of the trustees may ever be connected to a paid supplier.
6. **Groups we help.** If a trustee is involved with a group that applies for a free website, they declare it and take no part in the decision about that application.
7. **If in doubt, declare.** If anyone, including a trustee, member or volunteer, thinks there may be a conflict, they say so.

## Breaches
If a conflict was not declared, the trustees look at the decision again without the conflicted trustee. If needed, they put things right, and they record what happened. A serious or repeated breach may lead the other trustees to ask the trustee to resign, and it may need to be reported to the Charity Commission.

- We follow the **Wales Safeguarding Procedures** (safeguarding.wales) and work with Rhondda Cynon Taf's safeguarding services.

## Designated Safeguarding Lead (DSL)
The trustees appoint a DSL, and a deputy, from among the trustees. The DSL:
- is the first contact for any concern;
- decides whether to make a referral;
- keeps records secure; and
- makes sure training happens.

## Safe practice at drop-ins and sessions
- Sessions take place in public venues (libraries, community centres), never in someone's home.
- At least two people from Valleys Digital are present at every drop-in.
- **We never ask for, look at, type in or write down anyone's passwords, PINs, bank details or one-time codes.** We show people how to enter them themselves.

5. The DSL decides whether to refer the concern to the local authority:
   - Rhondda Cynon Taf adult or children's services; or
   - for a scam or fraud, Action Fraud (0300 123 2040).

6. If the concern is about the DSL, tell another trustee.
